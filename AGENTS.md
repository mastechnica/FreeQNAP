# FreeQNAP project rules

The owner approved publication on 31 October 2026 to the personal mastechnica/FreeQNAP repository and GitHub Pages from /docs. Use a clean reviewed source snapshot, not private development history. Keep all private state, originals, credentials, device captures and OS binaries out of public commits. Do not publish to another account or organization.

Keep the product focused on a locally managed NAS. The owner explicitly expanded scope to media discovery with per-client approval, OpenVPN/WireGuard client/server and VPN gateway/firewall support, and resumable URL/torrent downloads. The owner also authorized a multi-pane file manager with configurable recycle retention and optional private-LAN DHCP/client/firewall controls, disabled by default. The owner also authorized separately managed USB external data volumes, including a TR-004. Keep USB out of internal/boot RAID, require stable identities and fresh reviewed destructive plans, and do not auto-run sector-writing vendor SMART/RAID commands. Preserve default-deny boundaries, private credentials, reviewed changes and acceptance gates. Do not import EyeQ/Spectrum applications, camera/ONVIF/AI/access-control integrations, customer data, accounts, credentials, licenses or private source snapshots. No Node-RED, cloud fleet or telemetry service.

Never copy the old project Git history, .private directory, runtime captures or hardware flash/EEPROM dumps. Keep local device evidence in ignored .private only. Hardware ABI identifiers retained from the accepted kernel do not imply an EyeQ dependency.

Disk writes require immutable identities, a fresh reviewed plan and explicit confirmation. Protect the running root and all ancestors; no broad scan/assembly or automatic formatting. Fail closed on identity changes, active holders, mounted candidates, stale plans or partial operations. Backups must remain durable and independently verifiable after failure; retain the last good archive. Never silently restore pending application events or credentials.

State claims precisely: tests on fixtures/emulation are not physical hardware acceptance. Keep degraded/rebuild, USB SuperSpeed, SFP+, adaptive cooling, watchdog/wake and full-flash recovery limits visible. Preserve original license notices; do not redistribute binaries until corresponding source and license obligations are reviewed.
